# Figma Community research: awards, recognition and quote sections

Searches run on 2026-11-04 (no sign-in): "awards section", "recognition section", "testimonial quote section", "pull quote website", "achievements section portfolio". Extra searches: "awards list", "editorial testimonial", "awwwards portfolio".

Overall: Figma Community has very few files that are *only* an awards or quote section. Most hits are full site templates that include one, and the testimonial results are mostly the same card carousel. The picks below are the least generic. Each screenshot is the file's community page (cover or live preview), not the inner frames.

## 1. Elena Voss: Creative Portfolio
An editorial portfolio built on oversized type, strong contrast and a project-first layout. It has its own awards and testimonials sections next to selected work, services and FAQ. The cover shows overlapping project cards at different scales, so recognition sits inside the work story rather than in a separate badge strip. It's the closest match in tone to a high-end personal brand.

## 2. Award Recipient List (CAP25)
A gallery-style awards page: a centred serif masthead ("CAP25 / Award Recipients / Celebrating excellence in art"), then one large card per winner. Each card pairs a photo of the person with their work and an "Award recipient" pill, a big serif name, an italic quoted title, a short paragraph and a small-caps medium line. It treats every award as a short feature story, not a logo or a row in a list. This is the strongest concept for an "awards as editorial spreads" approach.

## 3. Award-Winning 2021 Portfolio (Hayden Bleasel)
A portfolio that won Awwwards recognition. Credibility is woven straight into running prose: client and employer names appear inline with their small logos ("worked with Google, Palantir, Nike, Toyota…"). A thin side column lists dated roles and recognitions in small grey type. It shows a quiet way to signal authority without a loud awards block.

## 4. Zyra Studio
A dark, bold agency template (Figma + Framer) with huge stacked wordmark type and a motion-blurred portrait as the hero. Its awards and testimonial blocks follow the same heavy-type, high-contrast system, so recognition reads as a type-led statement section. The file links a Framer remix, which is useful for seeing the scroll interactions in motion.

## 5. Brunet Studio
A Swiss/grotesk minimal studio site with giant "Creative Agency" type bleeding off the frame behind a browser mockup, monospace meta labels and red accent dots. Recognition and press appear as sparse, grid-aligned rows of label, year and detail. It's a good reference for a list-style awards table with strong typography.

## 6. Portfolio website, Awwwards style (Figma Make)
A live, animated Make prototype: a dark grid background, an "Available for new projects" status pill and a huge two-line hero ("Pure / Vision") where the second line is in an outlined serif. Interaction is the point: sections animate in on scroll. It's useful for motion ideas such as a counter or marquee of awards, but less for static layout.

## 7. Book Landing Page: Fever Pitch Book
An author/launch page that uses bold red and black poster panels, big pull-quote headlines ("What's at the H.E.A.R.T. of the perfect startup pitch?") and a "More praise for…" block of short endorsement cards with star ratings. It's the best pull-quote reference in the set: endorsements are presented as blurbs on a book jacket.

## 8. Client Loyalty & Impact Stats Section
A standalone recognition block: a centred heading ("Our commitment to quality…"), a short line of copy, then a row of four large number stats with small icons, including an awards count. The layout itself is generic, but it's one of the few single-section files that targets achievements directly. Use it as the baseline to beat (big numbers plus labels), not as the target.

## Takeaways
- The most distinctive idea is #2: each award as a full editorial card (portrait, name in big serif, quoted title, short story).
- For a quiet authority signal, #3's inline logos in prose plus a dated side column work well.
- For pull quotes, #7's book-jacket "praise" framing beats the usual testimonial carousel.
- Skipped: Awake Creative Agency (cover didn't load), Luxelab (paid, generic SaaS look), Architecture Studio landing page (no recognition section), and the many near-identical "testimonial carousel" files.

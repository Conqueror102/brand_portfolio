The future of law firms will not be determined by how much AI they adopt, but by how effectively they rethink the way they deliver value.
It was a privilege to speak International Bar Association (IBA) Annual Conference 2026 in Copenhagen, during the Law Firm Management Committee's World Café session, "Who Holds the Crystal Ball? Future-Proof Planning for Small and Medium-Sized Law Firms."
I had the opportunity to lead the roundtable discussion on Client Retention, examining how AI is reshaping client expectations, pricing models, talent strategies and, ultimately, the economics of legal practice.
Three key reflections emerged:
🔹 From billable hours to value: AI may reduce the time required to deliver legal services, but it does not diminish the value of professional judgement, risk management and strategic counsel. Are our pricing models keeping pace?
🔹 From relationships to relevance: Longstanding relationships are valuable, but no longer sufficient. Clients increasingly expect advisers who understand their businesses, anticipate risks and contribute to commercial outcomes.
🔹 From individual relationships to institutional capability: Sustainable client retention cannot depend solely on individual partners. It requires deliberate strategy, institutional knowledge, operational excellence and a consistent client experience.
These insights may appear straightforward, but their implications are far-reaching.
As clients increasingly integrate AI into their own organisations, they are not simply changing how they operate; they are also redefining what they expect from their external advisers.
For law firm leaders, client retention must therefore become a deliberate strategic priority, not an assumed consequence of good relationships or excellent legal work.
My appreciation to Norman Clark for moderating an engaging session, to Keith Heddle, Judit Budai and Yuri Sahione Pugliese, to my fellow speakers Sandra Bendler-Pepy, Dr Dadimos Haile, Daniel Koh, Elizabeth Heurtematte, Martijn Lesterhuis and M.  Georgia Gibson Henlin, KC, CIPP-E, CIPM, FIP for their perspectives, and the IBA Law Firm Management Committee for the opportunity to contribute.
I left Copenhagen reflecting on a question I believe every Managing Partner and law firm leader with growth ambitions should be asking:
If your clients were making their choice of legal advisers today, would they choose your firm again? More importantly, what would make them stay tomorrow?
Perhaps the real challenge is not simply retaining clients, but continuously earning the right to retain them.
